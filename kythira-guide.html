Kythira · Greece

An island where time
moves differently

◈

Hidden between three seas, Kythira rewards the unhurried traveller with wildflower valleys, empty coves, and villages unchanged for centuries.

The Coast

Beaches & hidden coves

From turquoise waterfall pools to wide sandy bays, Kythira's coastline remains one of the least crowded in the Aegean.

🌊

Fonissa Waterfall

A freshwater waterfall tumbling directly into a turquoise sea pool. One of Greece's most unusual swimming experiences.

Best visited in spring when the waterfall is at full flow.

🏖️

Firi Ammos

Remote red-sand beach accessible only on foot or by boat. The effort is entirely worth it.

Bring everything you need — no facilities.

⚓

Avlemonas

A small fishing harbour with crystal water and a Venetian castle watching over the bay. Perfect for an evening swim and dinner.

The taverna at the water's edge serves whatever was caught that morning.

The Villages

Where stone holds memory

Kythira's villages sit high above the sea, built inland to hide from centuries of pirates. Each one is a world unto itself.

🏰

Chora

The island capital, crowned by a Venetian fortress with views across to the Peloponnese. Whitewashed lanes, a fine archaeological museum, and the best sunset on the island.

🫒

Potamos

The island's largest village and commercial heart. The Sunday market draws locals from across Kythira — the place to find thyme honey, local cheese, and conversation.

🕌

Mylopotamos

A village built around a watermill and waterfall, hidden in a lush green valley. The medieval ghost-village of Kato Hora lies just below, half-ruined and haunting.

Walk down to the ruined castle — few tourists find it.

The Table

Eating & drinking well

Kythiran food is the food of a place that has never needed to impress outsiders. It is honest, seasonal, and quietly exceptional.

🧀

Local Specialities

Mizithra cheese unique to Kythira. Thyme honey from the mountain villages. Slow-cooked lamb. Fresh pasta made the way it has been made here for generations.

Ask for the local orektika — a spread of island appetisers.

🦑

Waterfront Tavernas

At Avlemonas and Diakofti, tables sit steps from the water. Order what arrived on the boat that morning — octopus, karavida, whatever the fisherman brought in.

🍯

Take Something Home

The thyme honey, preserved quince, and local wine from small producers in Potamos are worth seeking out. Nothing like it exists elsewhere.

Plan Your Visit

Everything you need to know

A few essentials before you arrive.

Getting Here

  • Flights from Athens (40 min) to Kythira Airport (KIT)
  • Ferry from Piraeus (7–8 hrs)
  • Ferry from Neapoli, Peloponnese (1 hr)
  • Book ferries early in July–August

Getting Around

  • A rental car is essential
  • Roads are good but narrow and winding
  • Limited bus service between main villages
  • Taxis available but scarce in remote areas

Best Time to Visit

  • May–June: Wildflowers, mild weather, no crowds
  • July–August: Peak season, warmest sea
  • September–October: Calm seas, local festivals
  • Winter: Quiet, authentic, many places closed

Good to Know

  • ATMs in Chora, Potamos, and Livadi
  • Many tavernas cash only
  • Siesta: 2pm–5pm, shops close
  • A few words of Greek go a long way
